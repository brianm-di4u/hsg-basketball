## 2. Defects ranked by exposure, with fixes

1. **Routine play still looks mechanically posed and crowded.** In `bursts/mobile-open-play.png` f00–f15, bottom-center paint, several defenders hold the same spread-arm stance while bodies bunch; desktop open-play f00–f15 lower-left shows the same congestion. Jumper f05–f08 on both viewports shows little distinct knee-load progression before release; f09–f17 returns toward upright without a strong landing compression. Ball separation is visible, so “ball never leaves the hand” would be false. Foot sliding is not established by these sampling intervals. **Fix:** add a deliberate load and landing compression, wrist articulation and varied defensive stance timing; improve spacing so silhouettes survive the paint clump. Preserve the readable release.

2. **Camera safety is improved only in selected situations.** `bursts/desktop-drive.png` f01–f02 puts the controlled athlete at the far-right edge, partly outside f01; f05–f15 leaves broad unused floor in the lower-left. Probe drive handlerInBandPct=40 and emptyFgMean=24.806 confirm an unresolved sequence-level problem, despite desktop c02 emptyFgPct=10.5 and open-play handlerInBandPct=100. On portrait, `stills/mobile/c11-mountain-pine.png` shows a full-court composition with players at probe median 53.5 CSS px; mobile drive f08–f15 center has a small shooter above extensive bare floor (probe emptyFgMean=36.831). **Fix:** constrain follow framing during drives as well as static play, and use a possession-aware portrait framing target. Do not sacrifice the basket or thumb separation.

3. **Small control text remains a recurring phone defect.** `stills/mobile/c02-live.png`, x440–690 y1500–1585, shows secondary STEAL/JUMP labels around 8 CSS px glyph height. Main PASS/SHOOT labels are much stronger in the same region. Desktop hub's small “next” badge and source line also occupy very little visual space (`stills/desktop/c20-hub.png`, x1010–1040 y433–445 and x240–560 y665–677). Pixel glyph height is an estimate, not a computed font-size claim. **Fix:** make secondary control labels at least 12 CSS px and enlarge supporting labels within an explicit type scale.

4. **Every make still risks gameplay occlusion.** `stills/desktop/c06-make.png`, x920–980 y495–550, has the exiting ball touching/disappearing behind the card's top edge. In `bursts/desktop-make-wide.png` f07–f09 lower center, the card covers the controlled athlete during possession transition; mobile make-wide f06 lower-center similarly places it over the nearby player's body. It starts fading around f11–f13, roughly 0.92–1.08 seconds after f00 at five ticks/frame, but possession has resumed earlier (f08–f10). Mobile f09→f10 also relocates the card sharply from midcourt toward the lower screen. **Fix:** fade on possession resumption, choose a position with player/ball exclusion rectangles, and stabilize the placement instead of hopping between available regions. Credit: both net-close strips f00–f11 have no slam crossing the ball, and mobile +3 is above the board (`stills/mobile/c06-make.png`, x360–460 y285–350).

5. **Make feedback is cleaner but crowd response remains ambiguous.** Both net-close strips f00–f05 upper center show ball passage and net extension; f04 onward shows a red rim, replacing the prior pale discs. Do not call this “no rim reaction.” Physical rim movement is not clearly established. Raised-arm baseline fans are also present on an airball (`stills/desktop/c07-miss.png`, x1090–1280 y260–650; `stills/mobile/c07-miss.png`, x0–780 y360–540), so the packet does not demonstrate make-specific baseline celebration. **Fix:** show a visible home-section change after the score and a contrasting miss response; retain the clean net passage and short rim cue.

6. **Select is still an overlong form.** `stills/desktop/c13-select.png`, y770–799, cuts the Quarters controls; mobile c13 x0–780 y1550–1688 cuts the away-team list and shows no start action. A still cannot prove scrolling is broken, but the initial viewport does not present a complete setup. **Fix:** split home/away selection into stages or provide a compact, visibly scrollable list and persistent start action. The navy recolor is a style improvement, not a layout fix. Hoop Land's benchmark menus keep clear bounded groups (`benchmarks/benchmarks-02.png`, top row, full panels).

7. **Close-camera payoff exposes unfinished athletes and enclosure.** `stills/desktop/c21-face-close.png`, x0–320 y160–440, cuts a large limb at the frame edge; faces at x420–810 y110–350 and hands below remain mannequin-like against detailed spectators. `c15-bench-close.png`, x50–650 y300–520, still has empty chairs. **Fix:** keep close cameras farther out until hands and faces support them, populate benches, and bring crowd and athlete rendering into one visual style. Compare the coherent character/environment treatment in `benchmarks/benchmarks-04.png`, playgrounds2-1 middle-right and playgrounds2-2 bottom-left; still benchmarks do not establish animation quality.
