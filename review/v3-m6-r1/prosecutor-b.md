## 2. Defects, ranked by exposure

1. **Persistent off-ball crowding and repeated defensive posture.** Every sampled open-play frame packs most players into the paint (`bursts/desktop-open-play.png` f00–f15, lower-left; mobile counterpart f00–f15, bottom court above thumb deck). Full-size `stills/desktop/c19-handler.png`, x270–450 y395–640, still shows a line of similarly leaning white defenders; x820–865 y455–480 shows the reaching hand meeting the handler's head. The ball remains visible, so this is not a claim that possession is universally unreadable. **Fix:** separate off-ball bodies, vary stance phase and reach direction, and keep defensive hands clear of heads. Keep the visible dribble and yellow control ring.

2. **Jumper and pull-up still hold a pose instead of finishing an athletic action.** `bursts/desktop-jumper-close.png` f09–f16, central shooter: raised arms and torso remain almost unchanged across 21 ticks between endpoints. f17 begins lowering the arms only at the landing. `desktop-drive-close.png` f10–f15, center/lower tile: overhead hold persists for another 15-tick span. The jumper does have a clearer planted dip by f02 (feet/ring and bent knees, lower-center), so “no anticipation at all” would overstate the evidence. **Fix:** develop that load, progressively extend through takeoff, lower the arms earlier, and show an unmistakable landing compression. Do not erase the readable f08 release.

3. **Dunk remains suspended and incompletely evidenced.** `bursts/desktop-dunk.png` f09–f12, right-side rim: the ball remains near/above the ring before the downward passage at f13–f14. f02–f15, dunker feet: no touchdown appears across 39 ticks between those samples. f15 still has the player above the floor. `desktop-dunk-close.png` f08–f15, left edge: the crop loses the player, so it cannot certify hand contact, a hang or a landing. **Fix:** tie throw-down to first hand/rim contact, release or hang for the required short interval, and capture touchdown within the burst. Repair the tracking crop. I do not infer a new body-through-rim defect from these images.

4. **Net response remains too small for the binding test.** `bursts/desktop-net-close.png` f00–f04 versus f08–f11, upper-center net; mobile counterpart f00–f04 versus f08–f11, upper-center: a small length change is visible, but no convincing widening or swing of the lower hem. On the desktop strip the rim-to-hem distance is roughly 80 tile px at peak versus roughly 72 settled, about 11%, below 15%. This is an approximate visual measurement, not a probe statistic. The capture already has the ball inside at f00. **Fix:** capture a pre-entry rest pose and show stronger lower-net deformation followed by recoil; preserve the rim flash and official's rising signal (desktop f00–f04, lower-left).

5. **Mobile finishing composition wastes the court and the score tag lingers at the basket.** `bursts/mobile-dunk.png` f04–f15, center/lower play area: long empty wood below the finish; probe 57.756% mean empty foreground. The 89.8 CSS px median supports adequate player size, so the old “tiny dunker” allegation is rejected. `bursts/mobile-make-wide.png` f02–f13, upper-center: +3 occupies the backboard/clock region well after the main card disappears at f05. It does not cover the rim itself in these samples. **Fix:** tighten the finishing camera and move the tag beside the score bug, expiring by f08. Keep the rim visible and controls outside play.

6. **The mobile make-card clearance still fails.** `stills/mobile/c06-make.png`, x535–585 y950–1010: trailing shoe ends around y985 and card begins y998, approximately 13 device px, below the required 32. This looks better than the previous verdict's ~4 px but is not closure. **Fix:** reserve the 32-device-pixel gap against the lowest player throughout the card animation.

7. **New crowd has depth, but close views expose crude silhouettes and uniform reactions.** `stills/desktop/c15-bench-close.png`, x20–710 y220–590: similar bench claps, slab-like white footwear, coaches with sharply segmented trouser/leg shapes; `bursts/mobile-make-wide.png` f07–f13, upper crowd band: many matching raised-V arm silhouettes. Their transition into celebration is visible, so calling the crowd static would be false. **Fix:** diversify reaction timing, elbow poses and seated posture; clean up the close-camera coach silhouettes. This also moves Authenticity and Art toward their milestone targets.

8. **Close-camera jersey and skin artifacts remain conspicuous.** `stills/desktop/c21-face-close.png`, x800–845 y550–630: bright gold strip bridges the arm opening with background visible beside it. `stills/mobile/c21-face-close.png`, x125–330 y800–1000: broad gold armhole panel; x330–420 y570–680 and x600–720 y710–850: pale patches across nose/cheek and shoulder. **Fix:** bind trim to the torso and eliminate intersecting skin surfaces in showcase cameras. The new crowd does not excuse these carried defects.

9. **Select footer still leaks content below its buttons.** `stills/mobile/c13-select.png`, x50–730 y1644–1688: “Cutter-” and “Jessieville” remain visible below BACK/TIP OFF. **Fix:** clip the scroll content to an opaque footer boundary. Milestone 7 may own the redesign; this carried defect remains open now.
