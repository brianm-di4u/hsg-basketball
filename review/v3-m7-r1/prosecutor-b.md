## 2. Defects, ordered by recurring player exposure

1. **Routine ball-handler contact reads as bodies and hands crossing rather than weighted defense.** In `bursts/desktop-open-play-close.png` f05–f12, central pair, the defender's reaching arm crosses the handler's ball/torso area while both remain upright; f08–f12 shows little upper-body response. This is visible contact staging, not proof of a collision-system defect. Give the handler a shielding turn and the defender a recoil/withdrawal pose, keeping the ball silhouette clear. The moving feet in f00–f07 prevent a blanket claim that the animation is static; sliding cannot be established reliably from camera-relative strips.

2. **The phone scoring indicator remains in the basket column too long.** `bursts/mobile-make-wide.png` f05–f13, upper center, shows +3 above the backboard; it remains at f08 and is still fading at f13. `stills/mobile/c16-crowd-reaction.png`, x290–400 y180–335, confirms the pill overlaps the upper backboard/support column. It does not hide the rim in this still, so “rim obscured” would overstate the evidence. Move it beside the score bug and expire by f08, as the binding verdict requests. The three-point card itself is clear of the basket in mobile make-wide f00–f04, lower center: do not conflate the two overlays.

3. **Shot payoff lacks a completed bodily finish.** `bursts/desktop-dunk.png` f08–f13, right-center, carries the ball toward and through the ring while the dunker stays airborne; f14–f15 still supplies no landing compression. `bursts/mobile-dunk.png` f08–f15, upper center, corroborates the suspended finish. The close crop loses the dunker after f10 (`desktop-dunk-close.png`, left edge), so it cannot verify the landing. Release on ring contact, shorten the hang and show bent-knee landing in the capture. The jumper does have a load (`desktop-jumper-close.png` f01–f04, knees) and ball separation (f08–f10, upper center), but its arms stay raised almost unchanged through f16. Add arm recovery and a stronger landing transition. Do not remove the readable release or follow-through altogether.

4. **Portrait camera scale varies too much.** `bursts/mobile-dunk.png` f08–f15 places the dunk in the upper portion with a large empty lower court. Probe empty-foreground mean is 57.75%; this is a framing weakness, not a thumb-overlap problem. Zoom/tilt for the finish while retaining basket and landing space. `stills/mobile/c09-arena.png`, x290–430 y740–970, also shows small overlapping opponents; probe median player height 57.6 CSS px versus 83.2 in mobile c02. Preserve the separated control deck (`c09`, y1305–1688).

5. **Sideline movement repeats conspicuously.** `stills/desktop/c15-bench-close.png`, x30–550 y210–530, shows adjacent seated players with matching forward claps. The identical gesture is the visible failure; one still cannot measure their animation phase. Give neighboring players different seated actions and independently timed claps, then capture a bench burst. The official at x700–990 y190–750 still has curved shoulder stripes, arms out and shorts over dark legs. Replace the lower garment with full-length trousers and use a relaxed idle or explicit signal.

6. **Supporting text needs optical comfort, not merely a nominal floor.** `stills/desktop/c20-hub.png`, x237–900 y540–680, compresses the bracket note and source line into narrow, small text, while the main heading and primary button read readily (x235–570 y45–350). `stills/mobile/c20-hub.png`, x80–700 y1040–1450, makes that copy substantially more comfortable. Increase desktop supporting text size/line spacing and reduce condensation where necessary. Exact CSS sizes, font loading, OFL compliance and credits are not independently demonstrated by the supplied images; no missing-license or missing-credit defect is alleged.

7. **Close presentation still exposes carried surface artifacts.** `stills/mobile/c21-face-close.png`, x325–430 y550–850 and x600–700 y810–940, has pale irregular patches across the nose/neck/shoulder. `stills/desktop/c21-face-close.png`, x790–840 y550–600, shows a projecting gold armhole piece. Separate intersecting bodies and finish the armhole surface. These are visible artifacts; the exact rendering cause is not proven.

The net is not inert: `bursts/desktop-net-close.png` f00–f08, upper center, shows elongation and recovery while the ball exits; mobile net-close corroborates it. The referee raises both arms and the house builds its reaction (`desktop-make-wide.png` f01–f06, right sideline/upper half). These support the 7 animation anchor. Net-close begins with the ball already within the net (f00, both viewports), so the carried pre-entry harness request remains unmet. Audio, input latency, real-time frame pacing and quality-governor transitions are not assessed; fixed-tick strips and the probe do not establish them. The webm was not played, and no claims about its timing or sound are made.
